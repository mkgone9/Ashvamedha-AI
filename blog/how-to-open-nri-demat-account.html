How to Open an NRI Demat Account — Documents and Process

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 September 2026⏱️ 6 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

The process is genuinely different from a resident Indian demat account opening, with an additional linked account requirement most first-timers don't expect.

The Documents You'll Typically Need

  • PAN card (mandatory)
  • Passport copy, including visa/residence permit page showing your overseas status
  • Overseas address proof (utility bill, bank statement, or similar, typically not older than a few months)
  • Indian address proof, if applicable
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Bank account details for the linked NRE or NRO account
  • FATCA/CRS self-declaration form

The Additional Step Most First-Timers Don't Expect: PIS

Unlike a resident Indian demat account, NRIs trading in the Indian secondary market (buying/selling listed shares directly) need a Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS) account through RBI's framework, linked specifically to your NRE or NRO bank account. This isn't needed for mutual fund investing, but it is required for direct equity trading — a distinction that surprises a lot of first-time NRI investors who assume the process mirrors resident investing exactly.

NRE-Linked vs NRO-Linked Demat Accounts

Your demat account will be designated as NRE-linked or NRO-linked based on which bank account it's tied to — this determines repatriation treatment for securities purchased and later sold through it. NRE-linked holdings are generally more straightforward to repatriate; NRO-linked holdings follow NRO repatriation rules (limits, CA certification for larger amounts). Decide this based on the actual source of the funds you'll be investing, not simply whichever seems more convenient.

Video KYC Has Made This Faster

Many brokers and depository participants now offer video-based KYC verification for NRI account opening, reducing or eliminating the need for physical, in-person verification that used to be more common. This has meaningfully sped up the process for NRIs opening accounts remotely, though document requirements themselves haven't changed — you'll still need all the same documents, just submitted and verified differently.

The Process, Step by Step

  1. Choose a broker/depository participant offering NRI accounts specifically (not all do)
  2. Ensure you already have (or open alongside this) an NRE or NRO bank account
  3. Complete KYC — document submission plus video or in-person verification as required
  4. If you intend to trade direct equity, apply for the linked PIS account through your bank
  5. Once approved, your NRI demat and trading account are activated, linked to your PIS-approved bank account for equity transactions

Common Delays Worth Anticipating

IssueHow to avoid it
Overseas address proof too oldUse a document dated within the last 2-3 months
Mismatch between passport name and bank account nameEnsure consistent name formatting across all documents upfront
PIS approval delayApply for PIS alongside demat opening, not as an afterthought once you're ready to trade
