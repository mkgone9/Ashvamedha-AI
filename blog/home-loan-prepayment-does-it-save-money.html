Why Prepayment Genuinely Saves Money

Interest on a home loan is calculated on the outstanding principal balance. Any prepayment directly reduces that balance, meaning less interest accrues for the remainder of the loan — the calculator above shows this precisely for your specific numbers, and the savings are often larger than people intuitively expect.

No Prepayment Penalty on Floating-Rate Loans

RBI regulations prohibit lenders from charging prepayment or foreclosure penalties on floating-rate home loans for individual borrowers — a genuine, borrower-friendly rule that removes one of the biggest historical objections to prepaying. This doesn't apply to fixed-rate loans in the same way, so check your specific loan's terms if you're on a fixed rate.

Why Earlier Prepayment Saves More

In the early years of a loan, a larger share of each EMI goes toward interest rather than principal (interest is calculated on the larger, still-high outstanding balance). Prepaying earlier reduces that balance while it's still high, saving interest over more remaining months than the same prepayment made later in the loan term would.

EMI Reduction vs Tenure Reduction — Ask Your Lender Which Applies

When you prepay, lenders typically offer to either reduce your tenure (keeping EMI the same) or reduce your EMI (keeping tenure the same). Reducing tenure while keeping EMI the same generally saves more total interest, since you're paying off the loan faster — but reducing EMI improves your monthly cash flow instead. Confirm which your lender defaults to, and ask to switch if you have a preference.
