Returning to India as an NRI? Plan Your Investments Before You Move

Moving back to India is not just a change of address. It can change how you think about your investments, bank accounts, retirement corpus, insurance, taxes and the currency in which your future expenses will happen.

The biggest mistake: treating the return as a banking exercise. Your investment plan should be redesigned around your new residence, future spending and complete global balance sheet.

Start 6–12 months before the move

Do not wait until you have landed in India. Start by listing every major asset and liability: Indian mutual funds and shares, NRE/NRO balances, foreign brokerage accounts, property, pensions, retirement accounts, loans, insurance and cash reserves.

Current tax and residency rules can be fact-specific. For returning NRIs, specialist tax planning may also be relevant for foreign retirement accounts and the transition to Indian residency. 1. Build your “before returning” balance sheet

  • Indian bank accounts and deposits
  • Indian mutual funds, equities and other securities
  • Foreign stocks, ETFs and brokerage accounts
  • 401(k), IRA, pension or equivalent retirement accounts where applicable
  • Foreign and Indian real estate
  • Life and health insurance
  • Loans and other liabilities
  • Expected retirement income and major future expenses

This simple inventory often reveals duplication: too much property, excessive cash, overlapping insurance or investments that no longer match the family's future.

2. Decide where your retirement spending will happen

A returning NRI should calculate retirement expenses in the currency of the life they expect to live. If retirement will be primarily in Hyderabad, Mumbai or another Indian city, the retirement target should be modelled around Indian living costs while still accounting for overseas assets and liabilities.

Do not assume that a stronger foreign currency automatically makes the investment plan better. Currency movements can help or hurt the eventual rupee value of overseas assets. The portfolio should be driven by future liabilities, not by exchange-rate optimism.

3. Review NRE, NRO and other accounts

Your banking structure should be reviewed when your residential status changes. Do not simply continue operating accounts under the assumption that nothing changes after you return.

The correct treatment can depend on your status, source of funds and applicable RBI/FEMA rules. Confirm the current requirements with your bank or qualified professional before changing or closing accounts.

4. Do not rush to sell foreign investments

One of the most common emotional reactions to returning is: “I am coming back to India, so I should sell everything abroad.” That can be a costly shortcut.

Instead, ask:

  • What is the tax cost of selling?
  • Do I still have liabilities in that country?
  • Is the investment part of my retirement plan?
  • What currency will I need later?
  • Will residency change the reporting or taxation?
  • Would selling create unnecessary concentration in Indian assets?

5. Rebuild the India portfolio around goals

Once your complete balance sheet is known, separate money into practical buckets:

Near-term
Emergency reserve, relocation expenses, home purchase, children's fees and other needs within a few years.

Long-term
Retirement and wealth creation where volatility can be tolerated.

Legacy
Assets intended for spouse, children or future wealth transfer.

This is more useful than simply asking for the “best investment for returning NRIs.” The right investment depends on which bucket the money belongs to.

6. Review insurance before you return

Check whether overseas employer cover continues after employment ends. Review Indian term insurance, health insurance, dependents and major liabilities. Returning permanently can change both the need for cover and the practical importance of India-based insurance.

7. Plan the tax transition separately

Tax residency and FEMA residency are not the same question. Your tax position can depend on the Income-tax rules, while banking and asset treatment can involve FEMA/RBI rules. Do not assume one automatically determines the other.

If you are returning from the US, UK, UAE, Singapore or another country, also identify foreign tax obligations that may survive your move. For example, recent reporting highlights that some UK tax and inheritance considerations can continue after a person moves to India depending on their circumstances. citeturn0news33

8. The 10-point return-to-India checklist

  1. Fix your expected return date.
  2. List every Indian and overseas asset.
  3. List every liability and recurring commitment.
  4. Estimate your first 12 months of India expenses.
  5. Calculate the retirement corpus required in India.
  6. Review foreign retirement accounts before making withdrawals.
  7. Review NRE/NRO and other bank accounts.
  8. Check insurance and employer benefits.
  9. Get country-specific tax advice before large asset sales.
  10. Only then restructure the portfolio.
