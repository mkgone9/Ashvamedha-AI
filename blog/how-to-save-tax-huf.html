How to Save Tax Using HUF — Complete Step-by-Step Guide (2025)

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 July 2025⏱️ 11 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Banjara Hills, Hyderabad

One of India's most underused legal tax structures is the HUF — Hindu Undivided Family. It gives you a second PAN, a second set of tax slabs, and a second ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit. For the right family, it can legally save ₹1–3 lakh in taxes every year. Here's everything you need to know.

What is a HUF?

A Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) is a legal entity recognised by Indian tax law. It is treated as a separate taxpayer — it has its own PAN number, files its own Income Tax Return, and gets its own tax slab and deductions.

Despite the name "Hindu," HUFs can also be formed by Sikhs, Jains, and Buddhists under Indian law.

Who Can Create a HUF?

A HUF is created automatically when a couple gets married. You can formalise it at any time by:

  • Being Hindu, Sikh, Jain, or Buddhist
  • Being married
  • Having a joint family — which includes you, your spouse, and your children

Unmarried individuals, widows, or widowers cannot create a HUF alone — there must be at least two members (husband and wife constitute an HUF).

How to Create a HUF — Step by Step

  1. Create a HUF deed — a written document declaring the formation of the HUF, listing the Karta (head, usually the eldest male) and all members. A lawyer can draft this for ₹500–2,000.
  2. Open a HUF bank account — take the HUF deed to any bank. They will open an account in the name "Manoj Kumar HUF" for example.
  3. Apply for HUF PAN — apply for a separate PAN for the HUF at the Income Tax department using Form 49A.
  4. Transfer assets to HUF — transfer income-generating assets (investments, FDs, rental property) to the HUF. Family members can gift money to the HUF (gifts from blood relatives to HUF are tax-free).
  5. File separate ITR for HUF — every year, file an ITR for the HUF separately from your personal ITR.

Practical Example — Tax Saving with HUF

Scenario: Manoj earns ₹20 lakh/year. He also receives ₹3 lakh/year in rental income. Without HUF, all ₹23 lakh is taxed in his name.

With HUF: The ₹3 lakh rental income is received in the HUF's name (property transferred to HUF). HUF pays minimal or zero tax on ₹3 lakh (after its own basic exemption + 80C deductions).

Tax saved: Roughly ₹90,000–1,20,000 per year in Manoj's 30% bracket on the ₹3 lakh shifted to HUF.

What Income Can Be Put in HUF?

  • ✅ Rental income from property transferred/gifted to HUF
  • ✅ Investment income (FD interest, MF returns) from assets in HUF
  • ✅ Business income if HUF runs a business
  • ✅ Agricultural income
  • ❌ Salary income — cannot be transferred to HUF. Salary is personal income.
  • ❌ Professional income of individuals — cannot be HUF income

Common Misconception

You cannot transfer your salary to the HUF to avoid tax. Salary is personal income — it is always taxed in your hands. HUF works for investment income, rental income, and business income — not salary.

HUF Limitations — Know Before You Create

  • HUF income is clubbed with individual income in certain situations (e.g., if the asset was originally owned by you personally)
  • For maximum benefit, assets in HUF should be from gifts received by HUF members (from relatives), ancestral property, or fresh HUF capital — not converted from your own personal savings
  • HUF partition later can have tax and legal complications if not planned properly
  • HUF members share the HUF assets equally — this can cause complications during inheritance or disputes

Should You Create a HUF?

HUF makes most sense if you have: significant rental income, interest income, or plan to run a family business. It's less useful if all your income is salary.

Always consult a CA before creating a HUF to ensure the structure works for your specific income and family situation.
