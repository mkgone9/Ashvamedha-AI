What is Term Insurance and Why is it Non-Negotiable?

Term insurance is the simplest form of life insurance. You pay a premium every year. If you die during the policy term, your family receives the sum assured. If you survive the term, nothing is paid. No maturity benefit, no investment component — just pure protection.

This simplicity is exactly what makes it powerful. For ₹10,000–15,000 per year, a 30-year-old can get ₹1 Crore life cover. No other product gives this much protection for this low a cost.

Why Not Endowment or ULIP?

Endowment and ULIP plans mix insurance and investment — and do both poorly. They offer inadequate cover for the premium paid and poor investment returns (typically 4–6% vs 12–15% from mutual funds). We will cover this comparison in a separate article. For now: term insurance for protection, mutual funds for wealth creation.

How Much Cover Do You Need?

The standard rule: 10–15 times your annual income. But we recommend a more precise calculation:

Cover needed = (Annual expenses × years till retirement) + (All outstanding liabilities) - (Existing savings and investments)

Example: 32-year-old, ₹10L annual income, ₹20L home loan, ₹5L savings, retires at 60.

= (₹6L expenses × 28 years) + ₹20L loan - ₹5L savings = ₹1.68 Cr + ₹20L - ₹5L = approximately ₹1.83 Cr cover needed. Round up to ₹2 Cr.

Claim Settlement Ratio — How Much Does It Matter?

Yes, claim settlement ratio matters — but it's often misunderstood. A 99.5% CSR means the insurer settled 99.5 out of 100 claims. The 0.5% rejected were usually due to fraud, misrepresentation, or policy exclusions — not because the insurer refused legitimate claims.

Any insurer with CSR above 97% is generally trustworthy. Don't choose a plan solely based on the highest CSR — look at the overall features and premium.

Key Features to Look for in a Term Plan

  • Waiver of Premium on Disability: If you become permanently disabled and cannot pay premiums, the policy continues for free. Essential add-on.
  • Critical Illness Rider: Pays a lump sum if you're diagnosed with cancer, heart attack, kidney failure, etc. Consider if you don't have a separate critical illness policy.
  • Accidental Death Benefit: Additional payout on accidental death. Low-cost, worth adding.
  • Income Payout Option: Instead of lump sum to family, some plans pay monthly income. Better for families who may struggle managing a large lump sum.
  • Increasing Cover: Cover increases annually by a fixed % to keep pace with inflation. Useful for long-term plans.

Online vs Offline — Which is Better?

Online term plans from the same insurer are consistently 20–30% cheaper than offline plans bought through agents. This is because:

  • No agent commission in online plans
  • Lower administrative costs
  • Customers doing their own research tend to give more accurate health disclosures

Buy online if you are comfortable with the process. If you need help understanding the policy terms or comparing options for your specific situation, an advisor can help you without selling you anything extra.

Common Mistakes When Buying Term Insurance

  • ❌ Underinsuring: Buying ₹25 lakh cover to save premium — this is insufficient for most families
  • ❌ Short policy term: Don't buy a 20-year plan at age 30 — coverage ends at 50 when you still have dependants
  • ❌ Hiding health information: This is the #1 reason claims are rejected. Disclose everything honestly
  • ❌ Multiple small policies instead of one large one: One ₹1 Cr policy is cleaner than four ₹25 lakh policies
  • ❌ Not telling your family: Tell your nominee about the policy, where the document is, and how to claim
