Home Loan in India 2026 — EMI, Down Payment, Tax Benefits & How Much Should You Borrow?

A home loan is not just an EMI decision. It can affect your cash flow, emergency fund, investments, insurance and retirement for 10–30 years. The right question is not simply “How much will the bank give me?” but “How much can I comfortably carry without weakening the rest of my financial plan?”

Quick rule: treat the bank's maximum eligibility as a ceiling, not a target. Your comfortable loan amount should leave room for emergencies, investing and other responsibilities.

1. How a home loan works

You borrow against a property and repay the principal plus interest through EMIs. The EMI depends mainly on loan amount, interest rate and tenure. A longer tenure usually lowers the monthly EMI but increases total interest.

2. EMI: the easiest way to understand the cost

For illustration, a ₹50 lakh loan at 8.5% for 20 years has an EMI of roughly ₹43,400. Over the full tenure, total payments are about ₹1.04 crore, so roughly ₹54 lakh is interest. This is an illustration, not a current lender quote.

Illustrative ₹50 lakh / 20-year loan: principal ≈ ₹50 lakh; interest ≈ ₹54 lakh.

3. How much should you borrow?

Start with your household cash flow, not the bank's calculator. List take-home income, existing EMIs, school or family commitments, insurance, monthly living costs, investing and emergency reserves. Then test the proposed EMI under a higher-interest scenario and after a temporary income disruption.

4. Down payment is more than the headline number

Keep cash aside not only for the lender-required contribution but also for stamp duty, registration, brokerage where applicable, legal or technical charges, interiors, moving costs and an emergency reserve. RBI housing-finance data shows that LTV limits vary by loan size and regulated entity; do not assume one universal percentage applies to every loan.

5. Fixed or floating?

Floating rates can move when the benchmark or lender spread changes. RBI rules require lenders to provide specified options at rate resets for covered equated-installment personal loans, including changes to EMI/tenure and, subject to applicable policy, switching or prepayment options. Read the sanction letter and loan agreement rather than choosing only from the advertised headline rate.

6. Home-loan tax benefits in 2026

For AY 2026–27, the Income Tax Department states that under the old regime, interest on a qualifying self-occupied house can be deductible under Section 24(b) up to ₹2 lakh, while eligible home-loan principal forms part of the combined ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C limit. Under the new regime, the treatment is different: the official guidance lists Section 24(b) interest deduction for let-out property subject to the stated rules, not the old-regime self-occupied ₹2 lakh deduction. Tax treatment depends on property, loan and taxpayer circumstances.

7. Prepayment: don't make it a reflex

Prepaying can reduce future interest, but using every spare rupee to reduce debt can leave you cash-poor. Compare the guaranteed saving from reducing loan interest with the value of liquidity and your other financial goals. For eligible floating-rate loans to individual borrowers, RBI rules restrict foreclosure/prepayment penalties; always verify your specific lender and loan terms.

8. The five-minute decision test

  1. Calculate the EMI.
  2. Add property and ownership costs.
  3. Keep an emergency reserve after the down payment.
  4. Stress-test the EMI at a higher rate.
  5. Check whether retirement and other goals can continue.
