Accredited Investor in India 2026 — Eligibility, Benefits & AIF/PMS Rules

“Accredited Investor” sounds like a premium investment category. In reality, it is a regulatory status designed for investors who meet specified financial criteria and are considered able to understand and bear the risks of sophisticated products.

Important: Accredited Investor status does not mean an investment is safe, guaranteed or automatically suitable. It changes which regulatory frameworks and investment options may be available.

What is an Accredited Investor?

SEBI's framework allows qualifying investors to obtain accredited status through an accreditation agency. The framework is relevant to products and services such as Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), Portfolio Management Services (PMS) and certain investment-service arrangements.

Who can qualify in 2026?

For individuals, HUFs, family trusts and sole proprietorships, SEBI's published criteria include any one of these routes:

  • Annual income of at least ₹2 crore; or
  • Net worth of at least ₹7.5 crore, with at least ₹3.75 crore in financial assets; or
  • Annual income of at least ₹1 crore plus net worth of at least ₹5 crore, with at least ₹2.5 crore in financial assets.

These are regulatory thresholds, not a recommendation that investors should seek products merely because they qualify. SEBI's current framework and accreditation requirements should be checked before relying on the status.

Does accredited status remove the ₹1 crore AIF minimum?

For a typical AIF, the general minimum investment threshold is ₹1 crore. SEBI's framework provides specific treatment for Accredited Investors, including AI-only schemes where no minimum investment amount is prescribed under the relevant framework. Large Value Funds for Accredited Investors have separate rules and substantially higher minimum commitments. Always read the specific fund's placement memorandum and applicable SEBI rules.

Does it mean PMS minimums disappear?

No. Accredited status should not be confused with a blanket exemption from every PMS requirement. PMS has its own regulatory framework, disclosures, fees and eligibility conditions. A product provider should explain which specific rule is being relied upon.

What it does NOT mean

  • It is not a SEBI quality certificate for an investment product.
  • It does not guarantee returns.
  • It does not make illiquid investments liquid.
  • It does not remove the need for due diligence.
  • It does not mean every AIF, PMS or private investment is suitable.

Before using accredited status

  1. Confirm your accreditation eligibility and certificate.
  2. Understand the exact product structure.
  3. Check lock-ins and liquidity.
  4. Review fees and performance methodology.
  5. Understand taxation separately.
  6. Keep sufficient liquid assets outside alternative investments.
