Pay 10, Get 30: How Guaranteed Income Plans Work

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 October 2026⏱️ 8 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

"Pay for 10 years, get income for 30 years, and get all your money back at the end." It sounds too good, so most people either buy it blindly or dismiss it completely. Here is exactly how these plans work, with the real numbers, the real return and the fine print.

The short answer

  • 10-2-30: pay 10 years, wait 2, receive a fixed income for 30.
  • Example at age 40: ₹5 L/year → ₹5,60,450 every year from 53 to 82, plus ₹50 L back, plus ≈ ₹53 L life cover.
  • Return ≈ 6.45% a year, tax-free within limits. Great for certainty, weak against inflation, and must be held to the end.

How it works in 3 phases

Remember 10-2-30: 10 years you pay → 2 years you wait → 30 years you earn. Then your premiums come back as a lump sum.
  1. Pay phase (10 years): you pay the same premium every year. Your life is insured from day one.
  2. Wait phase (2 years): you pay nothing, receive nothing. The insurer is still investing your money.
  3. Income phase (30 years): you receive a fixed amount every year (or month). It is written in the policy and does not change with markets or interest rates.
  4. Maturity: at the end, you receive a lump sum, in many plans equal to the total premiums you paid.

This is a non-linked, non-participating plan: "non-linked" means it is not tied to the stock market; "non-participating" means there are no bonuses. What you see is exactly what you get.

The real return (IRR), honestly

Running the exact cash flows above through an IRR calculation gives about 6.45% a year. Here's what that means in practice:

Compared withWhat 6.45% tax-free equals
FD for someone in the 30% slab≈ 9.4% taxable FD
FD for someone in the 20% slab≈ 8.1% taxable FD
PPF (7.1%, tax-free, 15 years)PPF is higher, but its rate can change and the limit is ₹1.5 L/year
Equity mutual funds (long-term)Funds have historically returned more, with ups and downs

So the plan is not a wealth-creation product. Its value is that the 6.45% is locked for 42 years. No FD, bond or small-savings scheme in India locks a rate for that long. If interest rates fall to 5% in the 2030s, your income doesn't change.

The fine print to check

  • Can you pay all 10 premiums? Stopping early hurts. Surrender values in the first few years are much lower than what you paid.
  • Is the income really "guaranteed"? Check that the benefit illustration shows it under "guaranteed benefits", not as "projected at 4%/8%".
  • Is the lump sum the full premium? Some versions return 100% of premiums, some less, some none (with higher income instead).
  • Tax: keep total yearly premiums across post-April-2023 non-ULIP policies within ₹5 Lakh, and life cover at least 10× the premium, for tax-free payouts. Details in our tax guide.
  • Inflation: the income is flat. At 6% inflation, ₹5.6 Lakh in year 13 buys what ₹2.6 Lakh buys today.
  • Insurer strength: the guarantee is only as good as the insurer. Prefer large insurers with high claim-settlement and solvency ratios.

Frequently asked questions

How does a guaranteed income plan work?

You pay a fixed premium every year for a set period (for example 10 years). After a short waiting period (for example 2 years), the insurer pays you a fixed income every year for a set period (for example 30 years). Many versions return your total premiums as a lump sum at the end. Life cover runs throughout the policy.

What is the return on a guaranteed income plan?

The internal rate of return (IRR) on a typical pay-10, get-30 plan works out to roughly 6–6.6% a year, depending on age and premium. It is tax-free when your total yearly premium across such policies is within ₹5 Lakh and the life cover is at least 10 times the premium.

What happens if I die during the policy?

Your nominee receives the death benefit, which is at least the sum assured (life cover). Depending on the plan option, the nominee may also continue to receive the remaining income. Death benefit is tax-free.

Can I stop paying premiums midway?

You can, but it is costly. If you stop after the minimum period (often 2 years), the policy becomes "reduced paid-up" with lower income, or you can surrender it for a value that is usually much less than what you paid in the early years. Choose a premium you can comfortably pay for all 10 years.

Is a guaranteed income plan better than an FD?

It locks a rate for 30+ years, while an FD rate can fall at every renewal. For someone in the 30% slab, a tax-free 6.45% equals about 9.4% from a taxable FD. But an FD is flexible and can be broken anytime; the plan is not.
