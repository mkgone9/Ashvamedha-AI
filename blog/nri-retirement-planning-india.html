NRI Retirement Planning in India: How to Build a Retirement Plan Across Countries

Retirement planning is more complicated when your salary is earned abroad, part of your wealth is in India and you have not yet decided where you will finally settle. The biggest mistake is treating retirement as only a corpus-number exercise.

Start with one decision: Where do you expect to spend most of your retirement years? Your answer affects currency, healthcare, housing, cash flow and the role of India-based assets.

Why NRI retirement planning needs a different approach

An NRI may have assets in two countries, family obligations in India, foreign retirement benefits and a possible future return to India. Inflation and spending may also occur in different currencies. A good plan therefore connects the two sides instead of optimizing them independently.

Step 1: Estimate retirement spending

Build the retirement budget first. Separate essential spending from lifestyle spending, and estimate healthcare, housing, family support and travel. If retirement will be in India, use realistic future Indian expenses rather than simply converting today's overseas salary into rupees.

Step 2: Decide the retirement country and currency exposure

If your future liabilities are primarily in INR, a portion of your retirement assets may need INR exposure. If you expect to remain abroad, moving too much wealth into INR assets can create unnecessary currency risk. The correct mix depends on your actual future spending.

Step 3: Map every retirement asset

  • Indian mutual funds and equities
  • Indian fixed deposits and other fixed-income assets
  • Property and expected rental income
  • Overseas pension or retirement accounts
  • Cash and emergency reserves
  • Insurance and expected benefits

Then classify each asset by liquidity, risk, currency, tax treatment and expected role in retirement.

Step 4: Plan for parents and dependents

Many NRIs support parents or relatives in India. Treat this as a separate cash-flow goal instead of assuming it will come out of retirement money. If support is likely to continue for many years, include it in the long-term plan.

Step 5: Review insurance before retirement

Life insurance needs usually change as liabilities decline. Health insurance becomes increasingly important. Overseas employer coverage should not automatically be assumed to continue after retirement or after returning to India.

Step 6: Plan the return to India early

If you may return, review your banking and investments before the move. Residency changes can affect account status, tax treatment and reporting. The exact implications depend on your facts and should be confirmed with qualified tax and regulatory professionals.

How much retirement corpus does an NRI need?

There is no universal number. A useful estimate depends on annual retirement spending, inflation, retirement age, expected lifespan, investment returns, pensions and other income, taxes and the country where you will spend retirement. A ₹5 crore corpus can be inadequate for one household and more than sufficient for another.

A practical NRI retirement checklist

  • Know your expected retirement country.
  • Estimate annual retirement spending in the relevant currency.
  • Separate retirement money from family-support goals.
  • Review Indian and overseas assets together.
  • Check insurance adequacy and portability.
  • Understand tax and reporting implications before major transfers.
  • Review the plan whenever residency or retirement plans change.
