Garuda Aerospace vs ideaForge — A Drone Sector Comparison

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 September 2026⏱️ 8 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

Two of India's most-discussed drone companies, one unlisted and one listed, look almost like mirror opposites financially. Here's the direct comparison.

The Two Companies, Briefly

Garuda Aerospace (unlisted) is a Chennai-based drone company operating an integrated model across hardware, drone-as-a-service, training, and maintenance, serving agriculture, defence, mapping, and industrial clients. ideaForge (NSE: IDEAFORGE, listed) is India's market leader in unmanned aircraft systems, historically more concentrated on defence and government surveillance applications, and has been transitioning toward a broader drone-solutions model.

What This Actually Shows

The two companies illustrate genuinely different risk profiles within the same broad sector. ideaForge's revenue volatility appears closely tied to its historical concentration in lumpy government defence procurement — when a contract cycle slows, revenue can fall sharply, as its FY25 numbers show clearly (a company can be a genuine market leader and still show this kind of swing). Garuda's more diversified revenue base across agriculture, industrial, and defence applications may partly explain its steadier trajectory, though it's also a smaller company overall, and smaller revenue bases can sometimes show artificially smoother percentage trends simply due to scale.

What Neither Comparison Point Should Be Read As

This is not a case for "Garuda is the better investment because it's more stable" or "ideaForge is riskier because of its swings." ideaForge's full disclosure means you can see and evaluate its risk directly — you know exactly what happened and largely why. Garuda's more limited disclosure means its actual risk profile is less visible, not necessarily lower — a smaller company with less public scrutiny could have volatility or concentration risks that simply haven't been reported in the same detail. Visibility and actual risk are two different things, and it's a mistake to treat "I can't see the risk" as equivalent to "there is no risk."

The Honest Takeaway

Both companies operate in a sector with genuine structural tailwinds (India's drone policy push, indigenous manufacturing incentives) and genuine structural risks (dependence on policy, procurement timing, and a still-developing regulatory environment for commercial drone use). Evaluating either one requires looking past the sector narrative and into what's actually disclosed — and being appropriately more cautious, not less, where disclosure is thinner.
