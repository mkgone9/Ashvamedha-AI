US Stock Investment Advisor in India: Do You Actually Need One?

Buying a US stock is easy. Building and maintaining a sensible US equity portfolio is a different problem.

The key distinction: a broker gives you access to the market. A specialist can help you decide what role US equities should play in your overall portfolio.

When professional guidance can make sense

  • You already have a sizeable US portfolio.
  • You are investing a large amount through LRS.
  • You own concentrated positions such as one or two technology stocks.
  • You need to coordinate US investments with Indian assets.
  • You are concerned about tax, reporting or estate-tax exposure.
  • You want a goal-based allocation instead of stock tips.

What a good US-market review should cover

Start with allocation, not stock names. Review your Indian equity exposure, US equity exposure, debt, cash, goals, time horizon and currency liabilities. Then assess whether the US allocation is genuinely diversifying or simply adding more technology and growth exposure.

What an investment specialist should NOT promise

Be cautious of guaranteed returns, “sure-shot” US stocks, daily trading promises or claims that one broker is automatically the best. US equities can fall sharply, and currency movements add another layer of risk.

Advisor vs broker

A broker primarily provides account access and execution. Investment planning is broader: allocation, research process, risk controls, rebalancing and tax coordination. Also verify the regulatory capacity in which any professional is providing a service and whether compensation creates conflicts.

Questions to ask before hiring anyone

  1. What exactly will you advise on?
  2. How are you paid?
  3. Do you provide research or only execution?
  4. How do you handle concentrated US positions?
  5. How do you coordinate with my Indian CA?
  6. Do you understand foreign-asset reporting and US estate-tax considerations?
