MSEI's 2026 Turnaround — What the Real Trading Volumes Actually Show

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 September 2026⏱️ 7 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

This is a genuine, documented turnaround story with real, specific numbers — including how genuinely small the starting point actually was. Both things are true at once.

The Real Timeline, Stated Plainly

MSEI (Metropolitan Stock Exchange of India, formerly MCX-SX) entered a prolonged stagnant phase from 2017 to 2024, with limited product development and negligible market share. The turnaround began in 2024 with a fundraise of approximately ₹240 crore from strategic investors including Groww, Zerodha, and Share India, followed by a considerably larger raise of approximately ₹1,000 crore in 2025 from Peak XV Partners, Share India, and Jainam Broking — earmarked for technology upgrades, data centres, broker onboarding, and ecosystem development.

The Actual Resumption of Trading — Genuinely Small Numbers

Equity trading resumed in January 2026 after regulatory approvals and system upgrades. On January 27, 2026, the first day back, MSEI recorded just 14 trades and approximately 286 shares traded — a genuinely tiny starting point that should be stated honestly rather than glossed over. Total capital market value traded came to ₹29.34 crore, actually a decline from ₹44.48 crore the previous year, reflecting how historically low cash-segment activity has been.

Why Both the Investment Story and the Small Numbers Are True at Once

A genuine ₹1,240 crore combined investment from credible names (Groww's and Zerodha's parent entities, Peak XV Partners) is a real signal that serious capital believes in the turnaround thesis. At the same time, 14 trades on a resumption day is an honest reflection of just how early-stage this actually is. Neither fact cancels out the other — reporting only the funding story without the volume reality, or vice versa, would be incomplete.

The Broader Currency Derivatives Context

Worth noting: total premium turnover across all Indian exchanges in currency derivatives declined 39.6% in FY26, following an 84.8% decline the prior year — largely due to regulatory restrictions limiting exchange-traded currency derivatives to participants with genuine underlying foreign-exchange hedging exposure. This is an industry-wide headwind affecting MSEI's currency derivatives segment specifically, not something unique to MSEI's own execution.

What a Genuinely Balanced Read Looks Like

  • Real, credible capital has backed this turnaround — not a minor or speculative amount
  • Actual trading volumes remain genuinely tiny by any standard, including MSEI's own historical activity
  • Broker registration numbers suggest real infrastructure-building is underway
  • A broader regulatory headwind is affecting the specific currency derivatives segment industry-wide
  • Whether this translates into meaningful trading volume over the coming years remains genuinely uncertain — this is an early-stage turnaround, not a completed one

The Bottom Line

The honest picture on MSEI in 2026 is neither "this is dead" nor "this is a hidden gem" — it's a real, well-funded, early-stage turnaround attempt with genuinely small current trading activity. Both the credible backing and the tiny volumes are real, verifiable facts worth holding at the same time.
