How to Take Care of Parents in India While Living in the US

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 November 2026⏱️ 10 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

You are in Dallas, New Jersey or the Bay Area. Your parents are in Hyderabad, Vijayawada or a small town in Telangana. The 10½-hour time difference makes every phone call about a fever or a bank problem feel urgent. Here is a practical system, built from what my NRI clients actually use, to take care of your parents from the US without being on call every night.

The short answer

  • Protect first: health insurance + an emergency fund + a local person who can reach them in 30 minutes.
  • Send money by bank transfer or US card: the 1% US remittance tax from 2026 applies only to cash-type transfers. Gifts to parents are tax-free in India.
  • Then replace monthly transfers with a fixed income for your parents that doesn't depend on your job or your visa.

1. Health cover first

The biggest financial risk for parents in India is a hospital bill, not daily expenses. A heart procedure or a knee replacement in a Hyderabad corporate hospital can easily cost ₹3–8 Lakh.

  • Buy health insurance in India, in your parents' names. US plans usually don't cover them in India. Since 2024, IRDAI has removed the upper age limit for buying health insurance and capped waiting periods for pre-existing diseases at 3 years, so it is worth applying even if they are in their late 60s.
  • Aim for a ₹10 Lakh base cover plus a super top-up. You can pay the premium from your NRE/NRO account or your US card.
  • If a parent is 70 or older, enrol them in Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY), which gives every senior citizen aged 70+ up to ₹5 Lakh of hospital cover a year, regardless of income. Use it alongside private cover.

3. Sending money: tax and rules

US side

  • Remittance tax: from 1 January 2026, the US charges a 1% excise tax on certain remittances, but only those funded with cash, money orders or cashier's cheques. Transfers from a US bank account or a US-issued debit/credit card (wire, ACH, Wise, Remitly and similar) are exempt.
  • Gift tax: in 2026 you can give up to $19,000 per person per year without filing anything (that's $38,000 for each parent if you and your spouse both give). Above that you file Form 709, but no tax is due until you cross the $15 million lifetime exemption.

India side

  • Money you send to your parents is a gift from a relative, and gifts from relatives are not taxed in India. Your parents pay tax only on the income it earns (FD interest, for example).
  • Send to your NRE account first and transfer to your parents from there, or send directly to their resident account. Keep the transfer records.
Tip: set up one fixed monthly transfer on payday instead of sending money whenever they ask. Parents often hesitate to ask, and a standing transfer removes that awkwardness.

Two ways to set it up:

  • In your parent's name (if they are within the insurer's entry age, often up to about 60): you gift the premium, they own the policy and receive the income directly. This keeps it simple on the US side.
  • In your own name: usually gives more income per rupee and a higher life cover because you are younger. But as a US tax resident, an Indian policy may need FBAR / Form 8938 reporting, a 1% US excise tax on premiums paid to a foreign insurer, and the income may be taxable in the US. Speak to a cross-border CPA first.

For parents who are already 60+ and have savings, the Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (8.2%) and Post Office MIS (7.4%) in their own names give income immediately. The full plan, step by step, is in how to retire your parents from the US.

Frequently asked questions

Is money sent to parents in India from the USA taxable?

In India, money received by parents from their child is a gift from a relative and is not taxed. In the US, gifts above $19,000 per person in 2026 need Form 709, but tax is due only after the $15 million lifetime exemption. Income earned on that money is taxable for whoever earns it.

Does the 1% US remittance tax apply when I send money to my parents?

Only if the transfer is funded with cash, a money order or a cashier's cheque. Transfers from a US bank account or with a US-issued debit or credit card are exempt.

Can I buy health insurance for my parents in India while living in the US?

Yes. You can buy it online in their names and pay from your NRE/NRO account or international card. Medical tests, if required, are done in India.

Can NRIs invest in SCSS or Post Office MIS for their parents?

NRIs cannot open these accounts themselves. But you can gift money to your resident parents, and they can invest it in SCSS (if 60+) or POMIS in their own names.

What is the best way to give my parents a fixed monthly income from the US?

Combine immediate income (SCSS, POMIS or FDs in their names, if they have savings) with a guaranteed income plan you fund for 10 years. The plan then pays a fixed income for 30 years, so the support no longer depends on your job.
