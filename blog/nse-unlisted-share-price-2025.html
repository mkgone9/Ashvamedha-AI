Understanding NSE's Business

NSE (National Stock Exchange of India Limited) is not just any company — it is the world's largest derivatives exchange by volume and India's largest equity exchange. Here's what that means in numbers:

MetricFY2024–25 Data
Total Revenue₹15,000 Cr+
Profit After Tax₹8,500 Cr+
Equity Market Share~70%
Derivatives Market PositionWorld's Largest by Volume
Daily Turnover₹400–600 lakh crore notional
Listed Companies2,200+

NSE operates through multiple revenue streams: transaction charges, annual listing fees, data licensing, co-location services, and clearing charges through NSCCL. It is not dependent on any single product.

NSE IPO — Current Status (2025)

NSE has been planning an IPO for several years. The process has been delayed primarily due to regulatory issues including the co-location controversy (where certain brokers allegedly received preferential access to NSE servers).

As of 2025, there is no confirmed IPO date. However, the company has cleared most regulatory hurdles and SEBI has indicated NSE is closer to listing than ever before. Most market analysts believe the IPO will happen within the next 2–4 years.

When NSE lists, it will be one of the largest IPOs in Indian history — comparable to LIC's listing. Pre-IPO investors will have significant liquidity at that point.

Is ₹2,090 a Fair Price for NSE Shares?

Let's compare NSE to BSE — the only other listed exchange in India:

ParameterNSEBSE
Market Cap (approx)₹10.5 Lakh Cr (unlisted)₹15,000–20,000 Cr (listed)
Annual Revenue₹15,000 Cr+~₹1,500 Cr
Market Share (Equity)~70%~30%
P/E (approx)~12x at ₹2,090~40x

NSE trades at a significant discount to BSE on a P/E basis despite being the dominant player. This is the unlisted market discount — buyers demand a lower price for the illiquidity risk. If NSE listed tomorrow at BSE's multiple, it would imply a valuation of ₹35–40 lakh crore — but that's speculative.

At ₹2,090, you're buying a world-class business at reasonable valuations with a potential IPO as the exit catalyst. The question is purely about patience and liquidity tolerance.

Risks of Buying NSE Unlisted Shares

  • No IPO timeline: NSE has not confirmed an IPO date. You may hold for 3–5+ years
  • Illiquidity: If you need money urgently, finding a buyer at your desired price is not guaranteed
  • Price volatility: OTC prices can move significantly without any publicly disclosed reason
  • Regulatory risk: Any new SEBI action against NSE could delay the IPO further
  • Premium pricing: At ₹10.5 lakh Cr market cap, NSE is already priced as a mature company
