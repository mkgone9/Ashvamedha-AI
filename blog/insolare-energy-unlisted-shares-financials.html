Insolare Energy Unlisted Shares — Financials, Peer Comparison, and How to Buy

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 September 2026⏱️ 9 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

InSolare Energy is a real, operating solar EPC company with genuine recent business wins — but its unlisted pricing data is some of the most inconsistent we've seen. Here's what's actually verifiable.

What InSolare Energy Actually Does

InSolare Energy is an Indian solar, hybrid, and green hydrogen EPC (Engineering, Procurement & Construction) company, incorporated in the 2008-2009 period and headquartered with operations spanning 19+ states. Reported figures place its installed project base above 500 MWp, with a meaningful operations & maintenance (O&M) portfolio and multiple patents filed. Its leadership includes Dr. Sunit Tyagi as Chairman and Managing Director.

Real, Recent Business Developments

Unlike some unlisted companies where verifiable news is scarce, InSolare has had genuine, reportable business activity recently: a repeat solar EPC order for a 50 MW AC / 70 MWp DC project in Uttar Pradesh, and a technology partnership with US-based Versogen Inc to jointly develop AEM electrolyser solutions for green hydrogen. These are real, sourced developments — worth more than any vague "growth potential" framing, because they're specific, dated, and checkable.

A Genuine Data Reliability Warning — Worse Than Usual Here

This is worth stating plainly: across different unlisted-share platforms, InSolare's reported financial metrics vary dramatically — EPS figures ranging from ₹0.22 to ₹115.87, market capitalisation figures ranging from roughly ₹29 crore to over ₹1,165 crore, and even different ISIN codes cited by different sources for what's presented as the same company. This level of inconsistency goes beyond the normal "prices vary across brokers" issue we flag for every unlisted stock — it suggests genuine data quality problems somewhere in how these platforms are sourcing or reporting InSolare's numbers specifically. Treat absolutely nothing about this company's stated financials from any single unlisted-share platform as reliable without independent verification.

The Real Listed Peer: Sterling & Wilson Renewable Energy

Sterling & Wilson Renewable Energy (NSE: SWSOLAR) is a listed solar EPC company and the most directly comparable peer, with fully public, audited financials that illustrate how volatile this specific sector can be:

Fiscal YearSterling & Wilson Renewable Energy — Net Profit/(Loss)
FY23(₹1,174.96 crore) — large loss
FY24(₹210.79 crore) — narrower loss
FY25₹85.55 crore — turned profitable
Q2 FY26(₹473.20 crore) — swung back to loss, due to a one-off arbitration-related charge

This trajectory — from a massive loss, to a narrower loss, to profitability, to a sudden large loss again from a one-time legal/arbitration charge — is a genuinely important illustration for anyone evaluating an unlisted solar EPC company like InSolare. Solar EPC is a project-based, contract-execution-heavy business where a single dispute or delayed project can swing results dramatically, even for an established, listed player. There's no comparable disclosure available for InSolare to show whether it experiences similar swings, which is itself the key risk point: you can see this volatility clearly in the listed peer, and you cannot see it at all in the unlisted company.

How to Buy — The Process

  1. Choose a broker and treat any displayed "current price" with real skepticism given the data inconsistency noted above — ask directly how they arrived at their quote
  2. Complete KYC — PAN, demat CMR, cancelled cheque
  3. Confirm lot size — reported minimums vary, commonly in the range of several hundred to a thousand shares depending on the broker
  4. Pay via bank transfer only — never cash
  5. Shares credited to demat, typically within 24-48 hours

Lock-in terms follow standard SEBI norms for pre-IPO share categories: typically 6 months from acquisition for venture capital/FVCI investors, no lock-in for AIF-Category II, and 6 months from any eventual IPO listing date for other investor categories including retail and HNI buyers.

What to Weigh Before Investing

  • Genuinely inconsistent financial data across sources — more severe than typical for this market
  • Sector volatility — illustrated clearly by listed peer Sterling & Wilson's swings between large losses and profit
  • No DRHP filed — no confirmed IPO process underway as of recent reporting
  • Standard unlisted illiquidity
