How to Choose the Best Term Insurance — 8 Key Factors Every Buyer Must Know

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 July 2025⏱️ 10 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

Buying term insurance is one of the most important financial decisions you'll make. A wrong choice could leave your family underinsured, or pay too much for features you don't need. This checklist covers everything.

Factor 1 — How Much Cover Do You Need?

The most common mistake is underinsuring. Use this simple formula:

Cover = (Annual expenses × years to retirement) + (All outstanding loans) − (Existing liquid investments)

For most salaried individuals in their 30s, this works out to ₹1–2 Crore. For business owners or higher incomes, it can be ₹3–5 Cr.

Practical Benchmark

As a quick benchmark: your cover should be at least 10x your current annual income. If you earn ₹10 lakh/year, minimum cover = ₹1 Crore. This is a starting point, not the final calculation.

Factor 2 — Policy Term: How Long Should Coverage Last?

Your policy should cover you until your youngest child is financially independent — typically until age 60–65 for most people. Don't buy a 20-year policy at 30 that ends at 50 — you'll still have dependants and likely a home loan at 50.

Recommended: Buy till age 65 minimum. Many term plans now offer cover till age 80–85 for slightly higher premium.

Factor 3 — Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR)

CSR = percentage of claims the insurer settled last year. Higher is better. Any insurer with 97%+ is generally reliable. Don't sacrifice significantly better features or pricing for a marginally higher CSR (e.g., choosing a worse plan because 99.5% > 98.7%).

Factor 4 — Solvency Ratio

Less discussed but equally important: the solvency ratio shows whether the insurer has enough reserves to pay all claims. IRDAI requires minimum 1.5x solvency. Avoid any insurer below 1.7x. Check at irdai.gov.in annual reports.

Factor 5 — Online vs Offline Purchase

Online term plans from the same insurer are 20–30% cheaper than offline plans sold through agents. The coverage is identical — the difference is the elimination of agent commission.

Buy online if: you're comfortable with the process, have done your research, and are healthy. Buy offline or through an advisor if: you have complex health conditions to declare, need help comparing options, or prefer face-to-face guidance.

Factor 6 — Medical Declaration — Be 100% Honest

This is the most critical factor. The #1 reason term insurance claims are rejected is non-disclosure of medical conditions. Declare everything: diabetes, hypertension, prior surgeries, family medical history, smoking, drinking.

If you declare a condition and the insurer accepts at a higher premium — that's fine. The claim will be paid. If you hide it and the insurer discovers it at claim time — the claim is rejected, regardless of how long you paid premiums.

Factor 8 — Premium Payment Frequency

Annual premium is always cheapest (no processing charges). Monthly premiums cost approximately 5–8% more annually due to frequency loading. If cash flow allows, pay annually.

Checklist Before Buying

  • ☐ Calculated exact cover amount needed (not just ₹50 lakh because it sounds big)
  • ☐ Chosen policy term to at least age 65
  • ☐ Compared at least 3 insurers on premium, CSR, and features
  • ☐ Disclosed all health conditions honestly
  • ☐ Added Waiver of Premium rider
  • ☐ Chosen online plan to save 20–30% on premium
  • ☐ Told your nominee where the policy documents are stored
  • ☐ Set a reminder to review coverage every 3–5 years
