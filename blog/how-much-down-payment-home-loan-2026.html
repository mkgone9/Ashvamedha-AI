How Much Down Payment Should You Make for a Home Loan in 2026?

A bigger down payment reduces the amount you borrow, but putting too much cash into the property can leave you without liquidity. The right amount depends on the property, lender requirements and your wider finances.

Down payment is only one part of the cash requirement

  • Borrower contribution toward the property
  • Stamp duty and registration where applicable
  • Brokerage where applicable
  • Legal/technical and lender charges
  • Interiors and moving costs
  • Emergency fund after purchase
Don't make this mistake: “I have enough cash for the down payment” does not necessarily mean you can afford the purchase.

What is LTV?

Loan-to-value compares the loan with the value of the property. Regulatory and lender limits can vary by loan size and institution, so don't assume that a fixed 80% or 90% rule applies to every transaction.

Three down-payment strategies

  1. Minimum practical: preserve more liquidity but accept a larger loan.
  2. Balanced: contribute enough to keep the loan comfortable while retaining reserves.
  3. Large contribution: reduce debt, but only if you still retain adequate liquidity and can continue key goals.

What should you protect?

After paying the upfront purchase costs, you should still have a meaningful emergency reserve and enough cash flow for insurance, household expenses and long-term goals.
