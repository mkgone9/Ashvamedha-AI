Best Investment Plans for Children in India (2026 Guide)

Every parent wants the same thing: that their child never has to give up a college seat, a career move or a dream because of money. The good news is that a child gives you something adults don't have, which is time. Even small amounts started now can become large. Here is how to choose the right mix of investments for your child, with the rates and rules that apply today.

The short answer

  • Protect first: a term plan on the earning parent. The best child plan fails if the parent's income stops.
  • Grow: equity mutual fund SIPs for big goals 10+ years away (college, higher studies).
  • Secure: Sukanya Samriddhi (8.2%, for girls), PPF (7.1%) and a guaranteed income plan that pays the child a fixed income from 18.

Step 1: Protect the parent

A child's financial future depends first on the parent's income. Before any child plan, the earning parent(s) should have a term insurance cover of 10–15× yearly income and the family should have health insurance. Many "child plans" sold in India are mainly expensive insurance; a separate term plan plus investments usually works better. See do you need a children's insurance plan? and term riders worth adding when you have kids.

Step 2: Know what goals will cost

Education costs in India have historically risen faster than general inflation; many planners assume around 8–10% a year. A course that costs ₹15 Lakh today could cost ₹40–60 Lakh in 13 years.

A guaranteed income from age 18

Here is an option many parents haven't heard of. You can buy a guaranteed income plan with your child as the life assured and yourself as the proposer. You pay for 10 years; the plan then pays a fixed income every year for 30 years.

Think about what that income does across the child's life:

  • 18–22: college fees, hostel, laptop: ≈ ₹9,700 a month.
  • 23–30: higher studies abroad, a cushion during the first job, wedding expenses.
  • 30–47: a second income for their own family, EMIs, their child's school fees.

Unlike a market investment, the amount and dates are fixed on day one. Add a premium waiver benefit if available, so the plan continues even if something happens to the parent.

Tax rules for investments in a child's name

  • Clubbing: a minor's income (FD interest, for example) is added to the income of the parent who earns more, with an exemption of ₹1,500 per child. This rule was Section 64(1A) and is now Section 99 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
  • Tax-free options avoid the issue: SSY and PPF interest is exempt, and payouts from a life insurance policy within the ₹5 Lakh premium rule are exempt, so clubbing doesn't create tax.
  • Mutual funds in a minor's name: gains are clubbed with the parent until the child turns 18; after that the child is taxed on their own income.
  • Old tax regime: SSY, PPF and life insurance premiums for children qualify under the ₹1.5 Lakh deduction; tuition fees for up to two children also qualify.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best investment plan for a child in India?

There is no single best plan. Most families do well with a mix: term insurance on the parent, equity SIPs for long-term growth, Sukanya Samriddhi (for girls) or PPF for safe tax-free growth, and optionally a guaranteed income plan for a fixed income from 18.

Is Sukanya Samriddhi better than mutual funds?

SSY gives a guaranteed, tax-free 8.2% (Oct–Dec 2026) and is ideal for the safe part of a daughter's goals. Equity mutual funds can grow more over 15+ years but are not guaranteed. Using both is usually better than choosing one.

Can I buy a guaranteed income plan in my child's name?

Yes. You (the parent) are the proposer and pay the premium, and the child is the life assured. Ownership usually transfers to the child at 18. Insurers set minimum ages and risk start dates for minors.

How much should I invest monthly for my child's future?

Work backwards from the goal. For example, ₹40 Lakh for college in 13 years needs roughly ₹10,500–₹12,500 a month in equity funds at 10–12% expected returns, or more in safer options. A planner can split this across buckets.

Is income from investments in my child's name taxable?

Yes, it is usually clubbed with the higher-earning parent's income (with a ₹1,500 exemption per child). Tax-free options like PPF, SSY and qualifying life insurance payouts don't add tax.
