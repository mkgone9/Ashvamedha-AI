NRI Financial Planner in India: What You Should Actually Plan

An NRI portfolio is rarely just a list of mutual funds, stocks or fixed deposits. Your income may be abroad, your parents may live in India, your property may be here, your retirement may happen in either country, and your tax and remittance rules can change when your residency changes.

The real question: not “Which investment should I buy?” but “How should my India-linked wealth fit into my complete financial life?”

Why NRI financial planning is different

For a resident investor, many decisions can be made within one country. For an NRI, the same decision can involve Indian tax rules, FEMA/RBI rules, banking status, currency exposure, overseas taxation and future residency. That makes coordination more important than simply finding another product.

For example, an investment that looks attractive in rupees may not make sense if your long-term liability is in dollars or pounds. Similarly, a property, insurance policy or mutual fund may need to be reviewed when you return to India.

What a proper NRI financial plan should cover

1. Cash flow and emergency reserves

Start with how much you earn abroad, how much you send to India, recurring family commitments, debt and emergency liquidity. Avoid investing every rupee simply because money is available.

2. NRE, NRO and banking structure

Your account structure matters for receiving income, maintaining India-linked expenses and moving eligible funds. Account treatment and repatriation rules depend on the nature of the money and applicable regulations, so confirm the current rules before acting.

3. Investments

Review mutual funds, equities, fixed income, property and other assets together. The goal is not to maximize the number of products; it is to build an allocation that matches time horizon, risk capacity, liquidity needs and currency exposure.

4. Retirement planning

Ask where you expect to retire, in which currency your retirement spending will occur, and how much income must come from India. An NRI returning to India in ten years has a very different plan from someone permanently settled overseas.

5. Insurance

Life and health insurance should be linked to actual responsibilities: dependents, loans, children's education and the years for which your income is required. Employer cover abroad should also be assessed for portability and adequacy rather than treated as permanent protection.

6. Tax and reporting coordination

Investment decisions should be made with the relevant Indian tax treatment and, where applicable, overseas tax obligations in mind. Tax residency, DTAA, TDS, capital gains and reporting can materially affect outcomes. Use a qualified tax professional for tax advice.

7. Repatriation and future residency

If you may return to India, your plan should anticipate the transition. Residency changes can affect account status, taxation, reporting and the treatment of investments. Planning before the move is generally easier than correcting a structure afterwards.

How to choose an NRI financial planner

Do not choose purely on the basis of “best returns”. Ask five practical questions:

  • What regulatory capacity does the professional operate under?
  • How are they compensated, and are commissions involved?
  • How much NRI-specific work have they actually handled?
  • Will they review the complete portfolio rather than sell one product?
  • Can they coordinate with your CA, tax adviser or lawyer when specialist work is required?

When should an NRI get a portfolio review?

A review becomes especially useful after a large inheritance, property sale, marriage, birth of a child, major salary change, return-to-India decision, retirement planning milestone or change in tax residency. It is also worth reviewing if you have accumulated many investments over the years without a single allocation strategy.
