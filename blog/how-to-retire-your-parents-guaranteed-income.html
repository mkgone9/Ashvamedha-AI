How to Retire Your Parents: A Guaranteed Income Plan

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 October 2026⏱️ 9 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

"I want my parents to stop working" is one of the most common goals I hear from young earners in Hyderabad. Sending money every month works, until your own EMIs, marriage and kids arrive. Here is how to turn that monthly transfer into a guaranteed income your parents can count on, even if your own life changes.

The short answer

  • First protect: health insurance + emergency fund for your parents.
  • Then bridge now with SCSS / POMIS (if they have savings) and your monthly support.
  • Then secure later with a guaranteed income plan you pay for 10 years, which takes over the monthly support for 30 years.

Step 1: Find the number

Sit with your parents and list what the house actually spends in a month: groceries, electricity, gas, phone, medicines, domestic help, temple and family functions. Most middle-class families in Telangana and Andhra Pradesh land between ₹25,000 and ₹50,000 a month for two people.

Multiply by 12. That is the yearly guaranteed income you need to build. Example: ₹30,000 a month = ₹3.6 Lakh a year.

Step 2: Protect before you provide

An income plan without health insurance is a bucket with a hole. One hospital stay can cost ₹3–8 Lakh. Before anything else:

  • Health insurance: a family floater or individual policy for parents, ideally ₹10 Lakh base plus a super top-up. Buy before 60 if possible; waiting periods for existing illnesses apply. See health insurance for parents in India.
  • Emergency fund: 6–12 months of their expenses in a savings account or liquid fund that they can access.
  • Your own term insurance: if they depend on you, your life must be insured. If something happens to you, the support should not stop.

Step 3: Bridge now

Your parents need money from this month. Two ways to cover it:

If they have savings (retirement benefits, a sold plot, FDs): for a parent aged 60+, the Senior Citizens Savings Scheme pays 8.2%. ₹30 Lakh in one parent's name gives about ₹20,500 a month. Post Office MIS at 7.4% gives ₹9,250 a month on ₹15 Lakh joint. These two alone can retire many parents.

If they don't: your monthly transfer is the bridge. Set it up as a standing instruction on salary day, so it's automatic and they don't have to ask.

The problem with the bridge: it depends on you, every month, for the next 25–30 years, through job changes, your wedding, a home loan and your children's school fees. That is why you need Step 4.

Step 4: Secure later with a guaranteed income

A guaranteed income plan lets you pay for 10 years while you are young and earning, then receive a fixed income for 30 years. When that income starts, it replaces your monthly transfer. Your parents' income no longer depends on your salary.

Think of it as: "Bridge now, secure later." Your salary carries them for 12 years; the plan carries them after that, whatever happens to your job.

At the end of the 30 years, Ravi also gets ₹25 Lakh back (his total premiums), which by then becomes his own retirement money. And for the policy term, his life is covered for about ₹27 Lakh, so the plan protects his parents in the worst case too.

The honest trade-off: the plan's return is about 6–6.5% a year, and ₹23,700 in 13 years will buy less than it does today. That's why SCSS and a small mutual fund corpus stay in the picture: they can be topped up for inflation.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to retire my parents?

Start with their monthly expenses. If your parents need ₹30,000 a month, you need guaranteed sources paying about ₹3.6 Lakh a year, plus health insurance and an emergency fund. With SCSS at 8.2%, roughly ₹44 Lakh would generate that; or you can build it over 10 years through a guaranteed income plan.

Can I buy a guaranteed income plan for my parents?

Yes. You can buy it in your own name and pass the income to your parents, or, within the insurer's entry-age limits and medical rules, in your parent's name. Buying in your own name usually gives better numbers and higher life cover because you are younger.

Which scheme is best for monthly income for my parents?

If your parents are 60 or older and have savings, the Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (8.2%) and Post Office MIS (7.4%) are the first stop. For income that must continue for 20–30 years, add a guaranteed income plan or annuity.

Should parents put their savings in mutual funds?

A part can go into conservative hybrid or debt funds for growth, but money needed for monthly expenses should not depend on market movements. Keep the essentials in guaranteed sources.

What is the first thing to do before retiring my parents?

Buy health insurance for them. Medical costs are the biggest threat to their income. Then build an emergency fund, then the monthly income.
