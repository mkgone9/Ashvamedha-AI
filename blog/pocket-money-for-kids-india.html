Pocket Money for Kids in India: How Much to Give by Age

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 November 2026⏱️ 8 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad
📚 Part of: Children's Investments guide →

Pocket money isn't really about the money. It's the first place your child practises the decisions they'll make for the rest of their life: spend now or wait, want or need, borrow or save. Here is a practical guide for Indian parents: how much to give at each age, how to structure it, how to use bank accounts and UPI safely, and a way to give your child a "lifetime pocket money" that pays them into adulthood.

The short answer

  • Start around age 5–6 with small weekly amounts; move to monthly by 11–12.
  • Use the 3-jar system: Spend, Save, Share. Add a "parent match" on savings to teach compounding.
  • From 10, a child can have their own bank account; teens can use UPI Circle with limits you set.
  • Want to go further? A guaranteed income plan in your child's name can pay them a fixed income every year from 16–18.

Why pocket money matters

Children who manage small amounts early learn that money is limited, that choices have trade-offs, and that waiting can get them something bigger. These are exactly the lessons adults struggle with when it comes to EMIs, credit cards and saving. A ₹50 mistake at 8 is far cheaper than a ₹50,000 one at 25.

*Starting points for urban middle-class families. Adjust for your city, your budget and, most importantly, what you expect the money to cover.

The rule that matters more than the amount: the more it covers, the more you give. If a teen now has to pay for their own outings and recharge, raise the amount and stop paying for those separately.

🧠 Remember it: "Age + responsibility = amount." Increase pocket money only when you also hand over a new responsibility.

Allowance, chores or both?

Fixed allowancePaid regardless. Teaches budgeting. Risk: feels like an entitlement.

Hybrid (recommended)A small fixed allowance + extra for "beyond normal" jobs, like washing the car or helping with a garage sale. Daily duties like making the bed stay unpaid.

For young children use real jars, so they can see the money grow. Put a picture of their saving goal on the Save jar. For older kids, the Save jar can become a bank account.

Parent match: for every ₹100 they keep in Save for a month, add ₹10–₹25. This is the simplest way to teach "interest" and "compounding" before they can spell either word.

Bank accounts and UPI for kids

  • Minor savings account: under RBI rules updated in 2025, children above 10 years can open and operate a savings or term deposit account on their own, and banks may offer them net banking and a debit card based on their own risk policies. Below 10, the account is opened and run by a parent as guardian.
  • UPI Circle: a parent's UPI app can add a teen as a "secondary user". You can approve each payment (partial delegation) or allow spending up to a monthly limit you set (full delegation). The teen pays from your account, within your rules.
  • Prepaid / teen cards from banks and fintechs let you load a fixed amount: good for kids who are ready for cards but not for unlimited access.
Safety first: teach your child never to share OTPs, UPI PINs or card details, not even with "a friend" or "the bank". Turn on spend alerts to your phone.

7 rules that make it work

  1. Pay on a fixed day. Consistency teaches planning.
  2. No advances (or, for teens, advances with "interest" from the next month).
  3. Let them make small mistakes. Spending it all on day one is a lesson, not a crisis.
  4. Don't top up when the money runs out early.
  5. Set one big saving goal at a time and track it on a chart.
  6. Talk about your own money choices: why you compared prices, why you waited for a sale.
  7. At 13–15, start a small SIP in their name and show them the statement every six months.

Frequently asked questions

How much pocket money should I give my child in India?

A common starting point is ₹20–₹50 a week at 5–7, ₹50–₹150 a week at 8–10, ₹500–₹1,000 a month at 11–13 and ₹1,000–₹2,500 a month at 14–16. Adjust for what the money is expected to cover.

At what age should kids get pocket money?

Around 5–6, when they can count and understand that money buys things. Start weekly with small amounts and move to monthly by 11–12.

Can a 10-year-old open a bank account in India?

Yes. Under RBI rules updated in 2025, minors above 10 can open and operate savings and term deposit accounts independently, subject to the bank's policies. Younger children's accounts are operated by a guardian.

Can kids use UPI in India?

Through UPI Circle, a parent can add a child as a secondary user and either approve each payment or allow spending up to a set monthly limit from the parent's account.

Should pocket money be linked to chores?

A hybrid works best: a small fixed allowance for budgeting practice, and extra money for jobs beyond normal family duties. Everyday help at home stays unpaid.
