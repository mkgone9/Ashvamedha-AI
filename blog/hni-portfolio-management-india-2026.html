HNI Portfolio Management in India 2026 — How to Structure a Large Portfolio

A large portfolio needs more than a list of investments. The main challenge is making sure the portfolio has a clear purpose, controlled concentration, enough liquidity and a reporting system that lets the family understand what it owns.

Core idea: portfolio management is about the structure around investments, not simply finding more investments.

Start with the family balance sheet

Before selecting products, map listed shares, mutual funds, PMS, AIFs, unlisted shares, real estate, business interests, cash, loans and other liabilities. Then identify which assets are liquid and which may take months or years to exit.

Use asset allocation before product selection

Where PMS can fit

PMS is a regulated portfolio-management structure in which a portfolio manager manages securities for clients according to the agreed mandate. SEBI's PMS framework has a minimum investment amount of ₹50 lakh for clients, subject to the applicable regulations and exceptions.

Where AIFs can fit

AIFs can provide exposure to strategies such as private equity, venture capital, private credit and other alternative strategies. The general minimum investment for an AIF is ₹1 crore, with specific regulatory treatment for Accredited Investors and certain structures. AIF liquidity, fees and risk can differ materially from listed investments.

Concentration is a major HNI risk

Wealth can look diversified while still being highly concentrated. Examples include a large promoter stake in one business, multiple properties in one city, or several funds that ultimately own similar sectors. Look through the portfolio rather than counting products.

Reporting matters

A useful HNI portfolio report should show total net worth, asset allocation, liquidity, concentration, performance, fees, taxes and major risks in one place. Without consolidated reporting, families can make decisions using incomplete information.

Five questions before adding another investment

  1. What role does this investment play?
  2. What is the expected holding period?
  3. How quickly can it be sold?
  4. What is the total cost?
  5. What happens to the overall portfolio if this investment falls sharply?

Reviewing a large portfolio?

A consolidated portfolio review can help identify concentration, liquidity and allocation issues before adding new products.
