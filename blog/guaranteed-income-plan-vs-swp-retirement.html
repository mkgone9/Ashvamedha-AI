Guaranteed Income Plan vs SWP: Which Is Safer?

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 October 2026⏱️ 9 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

A mutual fund SWP will probably leave you with more money. A guaranteed income plan will definitely pay you the same amount every year. For retirement income, "probably" and "definitely" are very different words. Here is how to choose, with real numbers.

The short answer

  • SWP (systematic withdrawal from a mutual fund): higher expected return, but income can run out early if markets crash at the wrong time.
  • Guaranteed income plan: lower return (about 6–6.5%), but a fixed, contractual income for 30 years plus your premiums back.
  • The smart answer for most families is both: guaranteed income for needs, SWP for wants and inflation.

What each option actually is

SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan) means you invest a lump sum in a mutual fund and withdraw a fixed amount every month. The rest stays invested and keeps growing, or falling, with the market. Your income is fixed only as long as the money lasts.

A guaranteed income plan is a non-linked life insurance savings plan. You pay a premium for a fixed number of years (say 10), wait a short period, and then receive a fixed yearly or monthly income for a fixed number of years (say 30). Many versions also return your total premiums at the end. The income amount is written in the policy document on day one and does not depend on markets.

Remember it as: SWP = "My money, market's mood." Guaranteed plan = "Insurer's promise, fixed in writing."

Same money, two paths: the numbers

Let's take a 40-year-old who can set aside ₹5 Lakh a year for 10 years (₹50 Lakh in total) and wants income from age 53 for 30 years.

Path A — guaranteed income plan: about ₹5,60,450 every year from age 53 to 82, plus ₹50 Lakh back at the end, plus life cover of about ₹53 Lakh during the policy. Internal return: about 6.45% a year.

Be honest about this table: in most market scenarios the SWP path leaves more money. If anyone tells you a guaranteed plan "beats mutual funds", they are not telling you the full story. What the guaranteed plan buys you is not a higher return. It buys you the removal of the bad scenarios.

The real risk: a crash in your first retirement years

Look at rows 4 and 5 again. Both have the same average return. In one, the money lasts with ₹85 Lakh to spare. In the other, it runs out at 74. The only difference is when the bad years arrive.

This is called sequence-of-returns risk. When you are withdrawing every month, a fall early in retirement forces you to sell units cheap, and those units are gone forever, so they can't recover with the market. A young investor doing a SIP actually benefits from crashes. A retiree doing an SWP gets hurt by them.

Indian markets have had falls of 30–60% (2008, 2020) and long flat stretches. Nobody can tell you whether your retirement will start in a good decade or a bad one. That uncertainty is exactly what a guaranteed income removes.

Who should choose what

A guaranteed income plan suits you if…

  • You (or your parents) have no pension and need a predictable amount for rent, groceries and medicines.
  • You lose sleep when the market falls 20%, and know you might stop an SWP at the wrong time.
  • You want income to start at a fixed age (say 50–55) without managing anything.
  • You are in a high tax slab and can keep total premiums within ₹5 Lakh a year, so payouts stay tax-free.

A mutual fund SWP suits you if…

  • Your essentials are already covered by a pension, rent or other fixed income.
  • You can stay invested through a crash without touching the money.
  • You want your income to grow with inflation over 25–30 years.
  • You need flexibility to withdraw a large amount for an emergency.

The combination most families need

The question is usually not "which one" but "how much of each". A simple rule:

Needs from guarantees. Wants from markets.
Add up your monthly essentials in retirement (food, rent, utilities, medicines, insurance premiums). Cover that amount with guaranteed income. Put the rest in mutual funds and use SWP for travel, gifts and lifestyle, and to fight inflation over time.

For example, if a couple needs ₹50,000 a month for essentials, a guaranteed plan paying about ₹5.6 Lakh a year (≈ ₹46,700 a month) plus a small Post Office MIS covers the base. Their mutual fund corpus can then stay invested for growth, and a bad market year means a cancelled holiday, not a missed rent payment.

Because the guaranteed income stays flat, plan to raise the SWP portion slowly over the years. In 20 years, ₹5.6 Lakh will buy roughly what ₹1.75 Lakh buys today at 6% inflation. That is why the guaranteed plan should be the floor, not the whole house.

Frequently asked questions

Is SWP better than a guaranteed income plan?

On average returns, usually yes: a balanced or equity-oriented fund has historically beaten the 6–6.5% that guaranteed income plans deliver. But SWP income depends on markets, and a crash in the first few years of withdrawals can make the money run out early. A guaranteed plan pays a fixed amount whatever markets do. Many retirees use the guaranteed plan for essential expenses and SWP for the rest.

What return does a guaranteed income plan give?

For a typical "pay 10 years, get income for 30 years" plan, the internal rate of return works out to roughly 6–6.6% a year. If the payouts are tax-free (total premiums within ₹5 Lakh a year), that equals about 9.4% from a fully taxable FD for someone in the 30% slab.

Can I lose money in an SWP?

The fund value can fall, and if you keep withdrawing during a fall you sell more units at low prices. This is called sequence-of-returns risk. It does not mean you lose everything, but it can shorten how many years the money lasts.

Which is better for a retired person with no other income?

A person with no pension needs their basic monthly expenses covered with certainty. Guaranteed sources (Senior Citizens Savings Scheme, Post Office MIS, annuities, guaranteed income plans) suit that base. SWP from mutual funds suits money that can ride out bad years.

Is income from SWP taxable?

Each SWP withdrawal is partly your own capital and partly gain; only the gain portion is taxed as capital gains. Income from a guaranteed income plan is tax-free when your total yearly premium across such policies is within ₹5 Lakh and the policy meets the life-cover condition.
