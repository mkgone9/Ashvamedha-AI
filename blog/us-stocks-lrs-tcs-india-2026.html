LRS Limit & TCS on US Stock Investments From India in 2026

Before transferring money from India to a US brokerage account, understand the Liberalised Remittance Scheme and the tax collected on qualifying remittances.

What is LRS?

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme is the RBI framework that permits resident individuals to remit foreign currency for permitted transactions, subject to the applicable annual limit and conditions.

Where US stock investing fits

Remitting funds from India to invest in overseas securities is one of the common ways residents obtain foreign-market exposure. Your bank and intermediary may ask for purpose codes, PAN and other documentation.

What is TCS?

Tax Collection at Source can apply to specified remittances under the Income-tax Act. The rate, threshold, exemptions and treatment can change, so investors should check the current rules for the financial year and the nature of the remittance rather than relying on an old social-media post.

Do not confuse TCS with a final tax on your US stocks.
TCS is a collection mechanism. Your final Indian tax liability depends on your income, gains, applicable rules and tax-credit treatment.

Practical checklist before remitting

  1. Confirm your resident/non-resident status.
  2. Check the current LRS annual limit.
  3. Ask your bank how the remittance will be classified.
  4. Understand the applicable TCS treatment before transferring.
  5. Keep remittance and broker statements for tax records.
  6. Coordinate foreign-asset reporting with your tax professional.

Why this matters for large portfolios

For sizeable investments, remittance timing, cash-flow planning, TCS and documentation can materially affect how much capital is available to deploy. Plan the funding route before selecting stocks.
