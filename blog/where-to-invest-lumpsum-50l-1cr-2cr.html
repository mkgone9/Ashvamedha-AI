Where to Invest a Lump Sum — Rs 50L, Rs 1Cr, Rs 2Cr Frameworks

✍️ Manoj Kumar📅 September 2026⏱️ 9 min read📍 Ashvamedha Finance, Hyderabad

The amount changes which product categories you can even access — not just how much you allocate to each. Here's the framework by tier, not a stock-picking answer.

What Actually Changes by Tier — Access, Not Just Allocation

The real difference between Rs 50L, Rs 1Cr, and Rs 2Cr isn't "invest more aggressively as the number grows" — it's that larger amounts cross specific regulatory minimums that open entirely different product categories.

TierWhat becomes accessible
Rs 50LFull access to mutual funds, direct equity, PMS threshold (Rs 50L minimum) becomes reachable
Rs 1CrAIF Category I/II/III minimum (Rs 1Cr) becomes reachable; meaningful PMS allocation possible alongside other holdings
Rs 2CrGenuine diversification across mutual funds, PMS, and AIF simultaneously without over-concentrating in any one

Rs 50 Lakh Framework

  • Emergency fund check first — confirm 6+ months of expenses are set aside separately before allocating this as pure investment
  • Core: diversified mutual funds — equity and hybrid allocation based on your actual timeline and risk tolerance, not a fixed formula
  • Consider PMS for a portion if you specifically want direct security ownership and are at the Rs 50L minimum — see our PMS vs mutual funds comparison for the real trade-offs
  • Debt component sized to your timeline — shorter goals need more debt allocation, not less

Rs 1 Crore Framework

  • Same foundational logic as Rs 50L, scaled — mutual fund core remains the base for most goals
  • AIF becomes genuinely accessible — Category II (private credit, PE) worth evaluating if you want exposure beyond public markets, understanding the illiquidity and capital-call mechanics covered in our dedicated AIF article
  • Tax-saving allocation — ensure 80C/NPS room is being used within this amount if not already maxed elsewhere
  • Consider whether GIFT City (dollar-denominated exposure) fits if you specifically want currency diversification alongside asset diversification

Rs 2 Crore Framework

  • Genuine three-way split becomes practical: mutual funds for liquidity and core growth, PMS for direct ownership and customization, AIF for a smaller allocation to private/alternative strategies
  • Concentration risk becomes the thing to actively manage — at this size, avoid letting any single fund manager, sector, or strategy dominate
  • Estate and succession planning becomes genuinely relevant at this size — a will and nominee updates across all holdings, not an afterthought

What Doesn't Change By Tier

  • Emergency fund and insurance adequacy come first, regardless of lump sum size
  • Goal-based allocation (what is this money actually for, and when do you need it) still drives the equity/debt split — a bigger number doesn't override this logic
  • Staggering a large lump sum via STP over 6-12 months remains a reasonable, common approach at every tier above, not just the largest

The Bottom Line

The tier determines which product categories you can access — it doesn't change the underlying discipline: goals first, emergency fund and insurance confirmed, then allocate across accessible categories based on your actual timeline and risk tolerance, not the size of the number itself.
